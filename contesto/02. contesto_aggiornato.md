## The vision (unchanged in substance)

A small "factory" of **software made to measure for small, concrete problems**: trackers, league
tables, small archives, tools for organising, replacements for spreadsheets or manual procedures.

The guiding principle: **small problems → small solutions**. A defined scope, no endless projects,
no infinite cycles of feedback, strong automation of the internal work, human intervention
concentrated on decisions and exceptions, the client the owner of their own accounts and
environments.

## The actors

- **The user / the client**: brings a need, validates the analysis and the demo, pays.
- **The driver**: our collaborator. They register from the showcase site's **Lavora con noi** page;
  they can be external. Two levels:
  - **An enabled driver**: whoever applies at registration and passes an **interview** becomes one.
    They supervise the projects: the ones they brought in (with their personal links) and the ones
    assigned by the *drivers pooling*. They validate the analyses, supervise the α-test and set the
    price in the consumption tier. They can attach **small discounts** to their links. In the data
    they are `enabled: true`.
  - **A driver who is not enabled**: registered, with no interview. They cannot follow clients'
    projects; they can be an ambassador and do autonomous work. In the data they are
    `enabled: false`.
- **The ambassador**: any driver, enabled or not, who invites people to **use webtools** with a
  link generated from their driver area. They do not follow the project: they take a part of the
  fee (see *The split*).
- **Autonomous work**: a driver, enabled or not, uses the service for a project of their own,
  paying for it with their own **tokens** (see *The price model*).
- **The AI components**: the preanalysis, the prevalidator, the analysis engine, the analysis
  helper, the developer.

## The architecture (from the sequence)

### The public interface
- **The showcase site**, with the **Lavora con noi** page: it describes the three ways of working
  with webtools (ambassador, driver, autonomous work).
- **The preanalysis ecosystem**: the client states the request in two ways:
  - **A self analysis prompt**: a prompt to be used with their **own AI tools** (at no cost to us);
  - **The preanalysis AI aided system**: a form assisted by our AI, with a **cost limit / payment
    per chat**.

### The backend
- **The analysis ecosystem**
  - **The analysis prevalidator** → the check on the scope. If the scope is not valid, the request
    goes to **REJECTED**.
  - **The analysis engine**: it produces the complete analysis.
- **The drivers pooling (policies)**: it assigns the project to a driver according to some
  policies.
- **The dev ecosystem**
  - **The developer** (AI) → the check for a *dev failure* → the **α-test sylos**.
- **The costs and revenues calculator**: it collects the costs and the revenues (connected to the
  payment engine).

### The driver's interface
- **The analysis helper** and the **analysis validation**: the driver validates the analysis. If
  they do not validate it, the request goes to REJECTED.
- **The driver area** (a dashboard and statistics): generating the links — ambassador links for
  every driver, personal ones (with a possible discount) for the enabled drivers —, the balance and
  the topping up of the **tokens**.
- Supervision of the **α-test sylos**.

### Release and payment
- **The DEMO SYLOS**: publication of the demo.
- **The payment engine**.

## The main flow

1. The user arrives from the site and does the preanalysis, with the self prompt or with the AI
   form.
2. The prevalidator checks the scope. If it is no good, the request is turned down.
3. The analysis engine produces the analysis.
4. The drivers pooling assigns the project to a driver, who validates the analysis.
5. **The user validates the analysis.** If they say no, the request is turned down.
6. The developer AI develops. In case of a failure there is a loop or an escalation
   **[to be confirmed]**.
7. The driver supervises the α-test.
8. The demo is published.
9. **The user validates the demo.** If they say yes, one moves on to the payment. If they say no,
   the request is turned down.
10. The payment is recorded and the costs and revenues are calculated.

[to be confirmed] In the new flow the user pays only after having seen and accepted the demo.

## The price model (NEW)

### The standard tier
- A **guaranteed price**, whatever the project's real consumption.
- The historical constraint of **400 €** is **less rigid** today.

### Extensions: shares / goodies
- **Shares / goodies** can be bought to obtain **extensions of the service** beyond the standard
  perimeter.

### The consumption tier
- The user can choose **from the start** to work by consumption, if they think the project is
  **simpler or more complex** than what the standard tier covers.
- At the demo they receive a **custom price**, set by the driver like this:
  **price = the project's real cost of consumption + the driver's share**.
- The user can **accept or refuse** the price.
- Unlike the standard one, this tier **really calculates the consumption**. The *costs and revenues
  calculator* is therefore central.

| | Standard | By consumption |
|---|---|---|
| The choice | the default | declared by the user at the start |
| The price | fixed and guaranteed | custom, communicated at the demo |
| The price's basis | the tier | real consumption + the driver's share |
| The risk on the consumption | ours | the user's |
| A refusal possible | at the demo | at the demo, on the price too |

### Autonomous work
- The driver (enabled or not) brings a project of **their own** and follows it themselves: the
  driver's share **is not added** to the price.
- It is paid **by consumption with the driver's tokens**, plus the **system fee**. The tokens are
  the credit of the driver's account and are there for autonomous work exactly.
- The **fee is always paid separately**, never in tokens: the tokens cover the phases'
  consumption; at the **demo** the driver buys the code paying normally, and in this case pays
  **the fee only**.
- At every step — **pre-analysis, analysis, development** — the system takes off the tokens
  consumed. If they are not enough, it notifies the driver by email and the pipeline stops; after
  the top-up the driver unblocks it.
- The code is the driver's: they can use it, resell it, do what they like with it. They can also
  delete it from our system, to have a semi-deterministic certainty of the code's uniqueness.
- In the pre-analysis form it is the «lavoro autonomo» checkbox, visible only to drivers: it rules
  out another driver's link or discount and the ambassador.

### The split
- **An enabled driver**: most of the single project's earnings goes to them; a small **system fee**
  is left to the system.
- **The ambassador**: **50% of the system fee** of the projects of the people they invited, if they
  go through (the demo accepted and paid for). In the project: `billing.ambassador_uid`.
- The discount on the fee for autonomous work, foreseen earlier, **does not exist any more**
  (removed on 2026-09-22).

## Open questions
- What happens if the user refuses the custom price? Does it go to REJECTED at no cost, or do they
  pay something anyway?
- Is the driver's share free or bound by a policy?
- Which shares / goodies exist concretely and how are they priced?
- How does the flow behave in case of a dev failure (a retry, an escalation to the driver, a
  refusal)?
- How do the pooling policies work?
- How much the system fee is worth, and whether it is the same for clients' projects and for
  autonomous work.
- How much the drivers' «small discounts» can be worth: is there a ceiling?
- How much a token costs and how it is topped up.
- How the enabling interview is conducted, and whether a driver can lose their enabled status.
- Deleting the code: when it can be asked for and what is deleted (the repository, the demo, the
  backups).
